# MAR retrospective findings (hindsight)

Patterns seen in `outcome.json` for the MAR weekly dates: 105 from 2008-01-04 to 2009-12-31. They are **labels and hypotheses**: never feed
them back into the method or the fact sheet. The batch was scored only after its point-in-time build was committed and pushed (6eb0401).
Outcomes were computed on 2026-11-09 from the closes posted to 2026-11-06; every date has its 6-month, 1-, 2- and 3-year horizons.
Medians of an even count take the upper middle value (`statistics.median_high`). The 15%/yr hurdle is +32.2% over two years. "Call held"
is `call_check_2y`: CHEAP should clear the hurdle or beat the benchmark, FAIR land within ±15pp of it, RICH miss the hurdle and lag it.

- **CHEAP held on 43 of 52 dates; the misses are January to March 2008.** The nine failures, 2008-01-25 to 2008-03-20, returned -10% to
  -27% over two years and trailed SPY by 3-9pp; one-year returns there were -48% to -61%. Every date from 2008-03-28 beat SPY over two years,
  and the 14 dates on the last FY08 guide (October to December) cleared the hurdle at +60% to +208%. Across the year one-year returns had a
  median of -18.0% (ahead of SPY on 32 dates) and three-year returns +18.8% (-6% to +142%, ahead of SPY on 49). Every date sat through a
  42-66% drawdown; the low close was $12.49 on 2008-11-20.
- **The floor did not hold early, as for RCL.** The price sat under the EPP floor (18-19x the guided EPS: $34-37 a share in the first half,
  $30-33 in the second) on 50 of 53 dates, yet the January-March dates lost a fifth: the guide the floor rested on fell from $2.05 to $1.65 during 2008 and FY09's
  first guide was $0.95, so the floor fell with the earnings. Unlike RCL, the dates under the floor still beat SPY on 43 of 50; the three
  over it (2008-02-01, 2008-02-08, 2008-05-02) returned -27%, -23% and +2%. Hypothesis, not a rule: a floor on the current year's guide is a weak anchor for
  a fee business whose incentive fees fall with hotel margins; test a floor on normalized or trailing-cycle earnings on other tickers.
- **The early-2008 BASE was the most inflated.** January's BASE was +18.5%/yr on the Q4 2007 fee guide (+14% to +17%) and a 3.0% buyback
  term; it fell to +9.2% from the Q4 2007 print and below zero from October. Seen afterwards, a guide from the last good quarter carried
  BASE at the top; hypothesis only.
- **The signals read the last good quarter, and ranked the wrong way.** The proxy composite was 2.70-2.90 on the Q3 and Q4 2007 prints
  (the worst entries) and 2.00 from Q1 2008. Across the 53 dates it ranks the return to the m12-24 average price at Spearman -0.73; the
  entry price ranks it at -0.94, the EPP gap at -0.95 and Ratio B at -0.88, the adjusted gap at -0.25. One stock through one crash mostly
  tests "buy lower".
- **The two-year price landed nearest BASE on 34 dates and BEAR on 19,** at 2.3-4.7 times the BEAR price and 0.37-0.79 times BULL: by 2010
  the stock had recovered to $26-43 from the $12.49 low.

- **CHEAP to the Q4 2008 print was the trade of the series.** The six CHEAP dates (2009-01-09 to 2009-02-06, and 2009-03-06) all cleared
  the hurdle at +112% to +207%. Every 2009 date beat SPY over one year (median +52.9%) and over three years (median +80.2%, +42% to +210%).
- **The trough-year guide read the recovery as RICH, a second time across tickers.** From 2009-02-13 the current-FY EPS was the FY09 range
  ($0.95, then $0.81), BASE carried the quarter fee guides' collapse (-20% to -34%) forward for two years (-17% to -29%/yr), and BEAR fell to
  $4-5. The 21 RICH dates of February to July returned +44% to +191% over two years (up to 115pp ahead of SPY); the call held on none. This is
  the lesson of RCL 2009 and SHW 2008 (a guided trough year read RICH), here driven by two inputs at once, the EPS anchor and a BASE built on
  a quarter's fee collapse. Hypothesis for the method, not a rule: test a mid-cycle or multi-year EPS anchor, and a BASE that does not
  extrapolate one quarter's guide for two years, on other cyclical tickers and periods; never fit either to MAR's outcomes.
- **From July 2009 RICH was half right.** The 25 dates from 2009-07-17 returned +6% to +78% (median +19.1%), the call held on 15, and the
  m12-24 average price was 22-87% above the entry on every one: the two-year endpoints (July-December 2011) caught a fall that the average
  price does not, so by the endpoint-robust measure the RICH call was wrong on all 25. The two-year price landed nearest BULL on 23 and
  XBULL on 2.
- **Within the year the adjusted gap ranked the outcome.** Across the 52 dates the adjusted gap ranks the m12-24 return at Spearman +0.88,
  the EPP gap at -0.95, Ratio B at -0.94 and the entry price at -1.00; the proxy composite at -0.34. The gap's ranking is again the anchor
  change: it fell from +0.82 to -2.47 as the EPS guide fell and the price doubled.

## The encoding calls, seen afterwards

- The calls listed in retro/README.md ("MAR") move classes only in 2009 and on 2008-05-02. Reading the 2009 "assumptions ... for internal
  planning purposes" as no guide would have moved 17 dates of 2009-02-13 to 2009-07-10 from RICH to CHEAP (14) or FAIR (3); those dates
  returned +44% to +191%. GAAP EPS in the history would have moved 2009-02-20 and 2009-02-27 (+191%, +183%) toward CHEAP or FAIR and
  2008-05-02 from FAIR to CHEAP. These are hindsight comparisons of alternatives decided before scoring; the calls stay, and changing a rule
  means a new method version tested elsewhere.
- Today's live profile of MAR (`data/business_profiles.json`, a 2026 judgement, recorded in `outcome.json` as profile drift) also files it
  DURABLE, risk 2; the kind changes only the class label, and the outcomes above come from the price state, which the EPS anchor and BASE set.
