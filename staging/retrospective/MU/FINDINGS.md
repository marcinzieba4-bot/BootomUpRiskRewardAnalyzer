# MU retrospective findings (hindsight)

Patterns seen in `outcome.json` for the MU weekly dates: 314 in all, 105 from 2008-01-04 to 2009-12-31 and 209 from 2010-01-08 to
2013-12-31, all Micron Technology, Inc. (NYSE: MU, NASDAQ from 2009-12-30). They are **labels and hypotheses**: never feed them back into
the method or the fact sheet. Each batch was scored only after its point-in-time build was committed and pushed (2008-2009: 504c033,
firewall result 483f3a2; 2010-2013: 5324605, controls and firewall result 03d226a); the outcomes were computed on 2026-11-17. Every date
has its 6-month, 1-, 2- and 3-year horizons. No split falls in any horizon and Micron paid no dividend in any of them (to the end of
2016), so prices are as traded and total returns are price returns. Medians of an even count take the upper middle value
(`statistics.median_high`). The 15%/yr hurdle is +32.2% over two years and +52.1% over three. "Call held" is `call_check_2y`: CHEAP should
clear the hurdle or beat the benchmark (SPY).

## Across 2008-2009

- **One price state for two years; the outcome followed the entry point.** CHEAP and BUY on all 105 dates, robust 8 of 8 on every one,
  while the two-year return ran from -43.6% to +316.4%. The call held on 80 of 105: all 53 of 2008 and the 27 of 2009 to 2009-07-10. The
  25 that missed are the last 25 dates, from 2009-07-17, after the price had more than tripled from its low. Across the 105 the entry
  price ranks the two-year return at Spearman -0.91 and the m12-24 average at -0.95, and the EPP gap at -0.89 and -0.95.
- Hypotheses only, to test on other tickers and periods and never to fold into MU's method or fact sheet: (1) for a company whose decade
  is mostly losses, the rules anchor on the last profitable year in the window, which can sit far from the earnings of the next upturn
  in either direction, so a CHEAP class there is a statement about the old multiple, not about the coming earnings; (2) a series whose
  anchor and window do not move for two years reduces the class to a constant, and only the price-driven metrics (the EPP gap, Ratio B)
  rank the outcomes; (3) the year-on-year signal composite read the right way in 2008 and the wrong way in 2009, as the quarters
  recovered after the price did.

## Across 2008-2013

- **The call held on 189 of 314 dates** (median two-year return +54.8%, range -43.6% to +503.9%; 181 cleared the hurdle, 192 beat SPY). The
  125 misses came in two runs, each after the price had risen several-fold from a cycle low: 2009-07-17 to 2011-06-03 (99 dates in a row)
  and 26 of the 34 dates from 2013-05-17 (13 of the 21 FAIR dates and all 13 CHEAP entries from the Q4 FY13 print). Across the 314 the entry
  price ranks the two-year return at Spearman -0.57, the proxy composite at -0.69 (the wrong sign), the adjusted gap at -0.53 (the wrong
  sign), Ratio B at -0.32 and the EPP gap at -0.02.
- **The models said DURABLE on every date; today's profile says CYCLICAL** (`profile_drift` in each outcome: "Micron: HBM/DRAM; memory cycle
  at a record", reviewed 2026-09-22). No 10-K of 2007-2013 called the business cyclical, so the rule gave DURABLE and the class family
  "COMPOUNDER AT LOW PRICE" to a company with four loss years (FY2007-FY2009, FY2012) and three excluded years (FY2006, FY2011, FY2013) in
  the decade FY2004-FY2013.
- Hypotheses only, to test on other tickers and periods and never to fold into MU's method or fact sheet: (1) for a cyclical with few clean
  years, the anchor is whichever profitable year last survived the exclusions; it can sit below the next upturn's earnings (2008, 2012) or
  at a peak that will not recur soon (FY10's $1.85 for 2010-2011), and the class cannot tell which; (2) a window holding two regimes (32-75x
  days on small EPS and 3-6x days on large EPS) makes the floor and the class turn with the window's composition, not with the business, as
  in the 2013 FAIR-to-CHEAP turn; (3) the year-on-year signal composite was the wrong sign in four of six years for MU (2009, 2010, 2011,
  2013): it rose with revenue growth and margins, which peak near the price peak in a memory cycle; (4) BEAR as flat EPS was not a floor in
  2010, when 51 of 52 two-year prices fell below it.
