# MU retrospective findings (hindsight)

Patterns seen in `outcome.json` for the MU weekly dates: 105 from 2008-01-04 to 2009-12-31, all Micron Technology, Inc. (NYSE: MU,
NASDAQ from 2009-12-30). They are **labels and hypotheses**: never feed them back into the method or the fact sheet. The batch was scored
only after its point-in-time build was committed and pushed (504c033, firewall result 483f3a2); the outcomes were computed on
2026-11-17. Every date has its 6-month, 1-, 2- and 3-year horizons. No split falls in any horizon and Micron paid no dividend before
2012, so prices are as traded and total returns are price returns. Medians of an even count take the upper middle value
(`statistics.median_high`). The 15%/yr hurdle is +32.2% over two years and +52.1% over three. "Call held" is `call_check_2y`: CHEAP should
clear the hurdle or beat the benchmark (SPY).

- **CHEAP held on all 53 dates, through an 81% fall.** Every 2008 entry beat SPY over two years (SPY's own two-year return from those

  to +225.7% (median +43.2%); 49 beat SPY and 20 cleared the three-year hurdle.
- **The anchor was a small, old year, and the earnings that came were far larger.** Every model carried FY05's $0.29: FY2006 was excluded
  by the mirror test, and FY2007, FY2008 and FY2009 were losses ($(0.42), $(2.10), $(2.29)). FY2010, outside the series, came in at $1.85

  two-year high, and the two-year price was above it on 15 of them.
- **The ranks ran through the price, and this time the composite had the right sign.** Across the 53 dates the entry price ranks the
  two-year return at Spearman -0.86 and the return to the m12-24 average price at -0.94; the EPP gap ranks them at -0.87 and -0.94 and
  Ratio B at -0.61 and -0.36 (the right signs: within one anchor both move with the price). The adjusted gap ranks them at +0.39 and +0.58
  and the proxy composite at +0.28 and +0.62, also the right signs: the composite was 2.15 on the entries of January to June, 2.65 from the
  Q3 FY08 print and 2.30 from the Q4 FY08 print, so the higher readings fell on the cheaper entries of the second half.
- **The two-year price landed nearest BEAR on 31 dates and BASE on 22.** The BEAR price was flat EPS at the 10th-percentile multiple
  ($4.29-10.18), above the entry price on 44 of the 53 dates (Ratio B negative); no two-year price came within half of the BULL price (at most 0.47 times it).

## Across 2008-2009

- **One price state for two years; the outcome followed the entry point.** CHEAP and BUY on all 105 dates, robust 8 of 8 on every one,
  while the two-year return ran from -43.6% to +316.4%. The call held on 80 of 105: all 53 of 2008 and the 27 of 2009 to 2009-07-10. The
  25 that missed are the last 25 dates, from 2009-07-17, after the price had more than tripled from its low. Across the 105 the entry
  price ranks the two-year return at Spearman -0.91 and the m12-24 average at -0.95, and the EPP gap at -0.89 and -0.95.
- Hypotheses only, to test on other tickers and periods and never to fold into MU's method or fact sheet: (1) for a company whose decade
  is mostly losses, the rules anchor on the last profitable year in the window, which can sit far from the earnings of the next upturn
  in either direction, so a CHEAP class there is a statement about the old multiple, not about the coming earnings; (2) a series whose
  anchor and window do not move for two years reduces the class to a constant, and only the price-driven metrics (the EPP gap, Ratio B)
  rank the outcomes; (3) the year-on-year signal composite read the right way in 2008 and the wrong way in 2009, as the quarters
  recovered after the price did.
